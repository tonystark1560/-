## Line 4 — definition (Frame 4)

**Delivery:** brisk, natural.

    Let's start simple. A bubble is when the price of something rises far above what it's really worth, because people expect the price to keep rising.

**Delivery:** brisk, natural.

    The classic example is tulip mania in the Netherlands in the 1600s, when some rare tulip bulbs reportedly sold for the price of a house. People weren't buying tulips to plant them. They were buying them to sell to someone else for more.

## Line 6 — loop (Frame 6)

**Delivery:** brisk, natural.

    Bubbles feed themselves. Prices go up, so people get excited. People get excited, so they buy. They buy, so prices go up. Until one day, there are no new buyers left. And then the whole thing reverses.

If investors pay a hundred dollars today for a company that earns one dollar a year, they're betting that profit will grow a lot, and fast. That's fine when the growth shows up. It's a problem when it doesn't. In a bubble, prices stop pricing in a great future, and start pricing in a perfect one.
