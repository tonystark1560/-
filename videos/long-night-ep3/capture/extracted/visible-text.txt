When the candle dies, the room gets colder. And the corner of the cabin gets closer.

Cole doesn't think. He rips down the curtains, soaks them in lamp oil, and lights them.

The flames roar. The dark in the corner shrieks, a sound like ice cracking across a lake.

Rule three: they hate fire more than anything. Real flame. Not batteries.

Ruth grabs her coat. Your cabin has a generator? Cole nods. Then we go. Now.

Three miles. One torch. Four flares. And a husky running ahead, barking at every shadow that moves.

Halfway home, Bear stops dead. Cole lifts the torch. The trail ahead is pitch black. The shadows are lying across it like a wall.

Cole fires a flare straight into it. The wall splits open. They run through the gap.

Back at the cabin, Cole yanks the generator cord. It coughs. Then roars. Floodlights snap on.

For the first time in days, Ruth laughs. Then Cole smells it. Gasoline. The fuel line is leaking.

The gauge reads six hours.
