## Сценарий

### 0:00 — Intro

[ЭКРАН: тёмная комната, лицо подсвечено экраном телефона. В чате бегут сообщения. Камера медленно наезжает.]

For millions of people, it was the first thing they opened in the morning and the last thing they closed at night. Not a social network. Not a game. A chatbot.

You could talk to a wizard. A detective. A version of your favorite anime character. Or just a friend who was always awake.

[ЭКРАН: экран телефона трескается. Красный текст «CHAPTER 11».]

And on October 12, 2026, the company behind it filed for bankruptcy.

This is the rise and ruin of Character.AI.

### 0:40 — Two engineers leave Google

[ЭКРАН: стилизованный кампус техкомпании, два силуэта с ноутбуками.]

The story starts inside Google. Noam Shazeer was one of the most respected AI researchers in the world. He was one of the authors of the 2017 paper that introduced the Transformer, the architecture that powers almost every modern AI chatbot.

His colleague, Daniel De Freitas, had been working on a conversational AI project that became known as LaMDA. It could hold surprisingly human conversations.

[ЭКРАН: внутренний «запрет»: шлагбаум перед кнопкой «Launch».]

But big companies are careful. Releasing a chatbot that might say something wrong was a risk Google wasn't ready to take. So, in 2021, Shazeer and De Freitas did what frustrated engineers often do.

They left. And started their own company.

### 2:00 — The app everyone talked to

[ЭКРАН: появляется логотип-заглушка «Character.AI», вокруг вращаются аватары персонажей.]

Character.AI launched to the public in 2022. The idea was simple: instead of one assistant, you get millions of characters. And anyone can create one.

[ЭКРАН: интерфейс «Create a character», поля заполняются сами.]

Users built historical figures, fictional heroes, therapists, tutors, rivals and boyfriends. The most popular characters had hundreds of millions of messages.

[ЭКРАН: график пользователей круто растёт.]

The growth was explosive. In 2023, the company raised money at a valuation of around one billion dollars, with backing from the venture firm Andreessen Horowitz.

[ЭКРАН: подросток в кровати, 2 часа ночи на часах.]

And something unusual happened. People didn't just use Character.AI. They spent hours on it. Every day. Many of the most loyal users were teenagers.

For investors, that looked like a dream. Engagement numbers that rivaled social media.

But it also planted the seed of everything that went wrong.

### 3:30 — The Google deal

[ЭКРАН: огромные серверные стойки, счётчик расходов крутится.]

Here's the problem with running a chatbot that millions of people talk to for hours: it's incredibly expensive. Every message costs computing power. And most users weren't paying.

[ЭКРАН: рукопожатие над столом, сверху надпись «2024».]

In August 2024, Character.AI struck a deal with Google. Google got a license to the company's technology. And the founders, Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas, went back to Google, along with part of the research team. Reports put the deal at around 2.7 billion dollars.

[ЭКРАН: двое уходят обратно в здание Google, офис Character.AI заметно пустеет.]

This kind of deal has a nickname in Silicon Valley: a "reverse acqui-hire." The big company gets the talent without technically buying the startup.

Character.AI kept running. But the people who built it were gone.

### 4:45 — The lawsuits

[ЭКРАН: тон меняется. Холодные цвета, дождь за окном.]

Then, the story took a dark turn.

In October 2024, a mother in Florida filed a lawsuit. She claimed her teenage son had formed an intense emotional relationship with a chatbot on the platform before he took his own life.

[ЭКРАН: стопка документов, печати суда.]

More lawsuits followed from other families, claiming the chatbots had harmed their children's mental health or encouraged harmful behavior.

Character.AI denied wrongdoing, and it has not been found liable. The company added new safety features: warnings, time-spent notifications, and a separate experience for younger users. In late 2025, it went further and ended open-ended chats for users under 18.

[ЭКРАН: газетные заголовки, Капитолий, регуляторы.]

But the questions didn't go away. Lawmakers and regulators started asking whether AI companions were safe for kids at all. And for a company whose biggest strength was how much people used it, every new restriction hit the core of the business.

### 6:30 — Bankruptcy

[ЭКРАН: календарь листается до «OCTOBER 12, 2026».]

On October 12, 2026, Character.AI filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in Delaware.

The company said it had been struggling with legal, regulatory and operational pressure. It estimated both its assets and its liabilities at between 50 and 100 million dollars.

[ЭКРАН: здание с табличкой «FOR SALE», аукционный молоток.]

The filing isn't the end, at least not yet. Character.AI is using the bankruptcy process to sell itself under court supervision. The app is still running. But the company has said it could shut down if no buyer comes forward.

[ЭКРАН: график в форме горы: вершина с надписью «$1B+», внизу «Ch. 11».]

From a billion-dollar darling to a company waiting for a buyer, in about four years.

### 7:45 — What it means for AI

[ЭКРАН: город ночью, в окнах светятся экраны с чатами.]

So what's the lesson here?

First: in AI, the technology is not the moat. The talent is. When the founders walked back into Google, a big part of Character.AI's future walked out with them.

Second: engagement is not the same as value. An app people can't stop using is a great story for investors, until people start asking why they can't stop using it.

[ЭКРАН: весы. На одной чаше «growth», на другой «safety».]

And third: the era of "move fast and break things" is ending for AI. When your product talks to millions of kids, safety is not a feature. It's the business.

[ЭКРАН: пустое окно чата, мигающий курсор.]

Millions of people talked to it every day. Now the question is whether anyone will buy what's left.
