# Nokia Ruled the World. Then the iPhone Happened.

**Канал:** Rise & Ruin · **Формат:** 16:9 · **Длина:** около 9–10 минут (примерно 1400 слов)
**Превью:** неубиваемая Nokia с трещиной, рядом сияет силуэт смартфона без кнопок, «HOW?!»

## Описание для YouTube

> Nokia was once the biggest phone maker on Earth and one of Europe's most valuable companies. In less than a decade after the iPhone launched, it sold its phone business to Microsoft. From rubber boots to Snake to the "burning platform": the rise and fall of Nokia.

## Сценарий

### 0:00 — Intro

[ЭКРАН: телефон падает на асфальт, подпрыгивает и остаётся целым.]

It survived drops, floods and years in your pocket. It had Snake. And that ringtone everyone on the planet knew.

[ЭКРАН: карта мира заполняется синими телефонами.]

At its peak, Nokia was the biggest phone company in the world, by far.

[ЭКРАН: тот же телефон медленно покрывается пылью.]

And then, in just a few years, it lost almost everything. This is the story of Nokia.

### 0:45 — Paper, rubber and cables

[ЭКРАН: Финляндия, XIX век, бумажная фабрика у реки.]

Nokia didn't start with phones. It started in 1865 as a paper mill in Finland, named after a town by the river Nokia.

[ЭКРАН: резиновые сапоги, шины, кабели.]

Over the next century, it merged with a rubber company and a cable company. Nokia made rubber boots, tires, cables, even TVs.

[ЭКРАН: хаотичная схема из десятков бизнесов.]

By the 1980s, it was a messy conglomerate doing a bit of everything. And it was in trouble.

### 2:00 — The mobile bet

[ЭКРАН: 1992 год, новый CEO смотрит на ранний мобильный телефон.]

In 1992, Nokia got a new CEO, Jorma Ollila. He made a huge bet: forget the boots and tires. Focus on telecommunications. Specifically, mobile phones.

[ЭКРАН: вышки GSM загораются по Европе.]

The timing was perfect. Europe was adopting a new digital mobile standard called GSM, and Nokia was right at the center of it.

[ЭКРАН: продают сапоги, кабели, ТВ; остаётся только телефон.]

Nokia sold off its other businesses. Everything went into phones.

### 3:30 — King of phones

[ЭКРАН: золотые годы, яркие цвета, коллекция культовых Nokia.]

It worked spectacularly. By 1998, Nokia was the biggest mobile phone maker in the world.

[ЭКРАН: змейка ползёт по монохромному экрану.]

The phones were cheap, tough and easy to use. Snake turned millions of people into mobile gamers. The 3310 became a legend.

[ЭКРАН: фондовый график Nokia взлетает.]

Around 2000, Nokia was one of the most valuable companies in Europe. A huge share of Finland's exports came from one company.

[ЭКРАН: круговая диаграмма, синий сектор почти половина.]

And as late as 2007, Nokia sold roughly four out of every ten phones on Earth. No one was close.

### 4:45 — 2007

[ЭКРАН: сцена, человек в чёрной водолазке достаёт телефон без кнопок.]

In January 2007, Apple announced the iPhone.

[ЭКРАН: руководители Nokia смотрят трансляцию, пожимают плечами.]

At first, plenty of people in the industry weren't impressed. It was expensive. It had no physical keyboard. The battery life was short. It didn't even support the faster 3G networks at launch.

[ЭКРАН: тесты на прочность: Nokia выдерживает, iPhone трескается.]

Nokia's engineers knew how to build tough, reliable phones. And by those old measures, the iPhone didn't look like a threat.

[ЭКРАН: появляется сетка приложений и сенсорный палец.]

But the iPhone wasn't competing on toughness. It changed what a phone was for: the internet, apps, touchscreen. A computer in your pocket.

### 6:00 — The software problem

[ЭКРАН: внутри Nokia лабиринт команд, каждая со своим кодом.]

Nokia's phones ran on an operating system called Symbian. It had worked well for older phones. But it was hard to build modern apps for, and it couldn't keep up with the iPhone's smooth experience.

[ЭКРАН: появляется зелёный робот Android, телефоны многих брендов.]

Then, in 2008, Google's Android arrived. Suddenly, Samsung, HTC and many others could build smartphones using free, modern software.

[ЭКРАН: синий сектор Nokia в диаграмме тает.]

Nokia was squeezed from both sides: Apple at the top, Android everywhere else. Its internal teams worked on several competing software projects. Decisions were slow. Its smartphone market share started collapsing.

### 7:15 — The burning platform

[ЭКРАН: 2010, новый CEO из Microsoft, Стивен Элоп.]

In 2010, Nokia hired a new CEO from Microsoft, Stephen Elop.

[ЭКРАН: горящая нефтяная платформа в море, человек на краю.]

In early 2011, he sent a famous internal memo. He compared Nokia to a man standing on a burning oil platform, forced to jump into the freezing sea.

[ЭКРАН: рукопожатие Nokia и Microsoft, логотип Windows Phone.]

Nokia's jump: dropping its own smartphone software and betting on Microsoft's Windows Phone.

[ЭКРАН: красивые разноцветные Lumia на полке, покупателей нет.]

The new Lumia phones looked great. But Windows Phone had a big problem: few apps. Customers had already chosen iPhone or Android.

### 8:30 — Sold

[ЭКРАН: 2013, чемодан с логотипом Nokia передают Microsoft.]

In 2013, Nokia agreed to sell its phone business to Microsoft for around 5.4 billion euros.

[ЭКРАН: Microsoft через пару лет списывает покупку, заводы закрываются.]

It didn't work out for Microsoft either. Within a couple of years, Microsoft wrote off most of the deal, and eventually gave up on its phones.

[ЭКРАН: современная Nokia: сетевые вышки, 5G.]

Nokia itself survived. Today, it's mainly a telecom equipment company, building the networks phones run on. It came back, quietly. But it never ruled your pocket again.

[ЭКРАН: корона медленно соскальзывает с синего телефона.]

Nokia proves that being number one is temporary. The biggest danger isn't a competitor that beats you at your own game. It's one that changes the game.
