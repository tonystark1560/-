# Kodak Invented the Digital Camera… and Still Died

**Канал:** Rise & Ruin · **Формат:** 16:9 · **Длина:** около 9–10 минут (примерно 1400 слов)
**Превью:** жёлтая коробка плёнки, из неё выпадает цифровая камера, тень камеры похожа на надгробие, «THEY INVENTED IT».

## Описание для YouTube

> In 1975, a Kodak engineer built the world's first digital camera. Kodak's response: don't tell anyone. This is the story of how the company that defined photography for a century invented its own replacement and still went bankrupt.

## Сценарий

### 0:00 — Intro

[ЭКРАН: семейный альбом листается, тёплые жёлтые тона.]

For more than a hundred years, if you took a photo, there's a good chance Kodak was involved. The film. The camera. The paper it was printed on.

[ЭКРАН: лаборатория 1975 года, вспышка, странный прибор.]

And in 1975, a young Kodak engineer built something that would change photography forever: the first digital camera.

[ЭКРАН: прибор кладут в ящик, ящик закрывается.]

Kodak invented the future. And then it put it in a drawer.

### 0:45 — You press the button

[ЭКРАН: Рочестер, конец XIX века. Мужчина в котелке.]

Kodak was founded by George Eastman in Rochester, New York. In 1888, he sold the first Kodak camera with a brilliant slogan: "You press the button, we do the rest."

[ЭКРАН: схема: камера → почта → фабрика → фото.]

Before that, photography was complicated, expensive and messy. Eastman made it simple. You took pictures, mailed the camera back, and Kodak developed your photos.

[ЭКРАН: детская камера «Brownie» за $1.]

In 1900, Kodak launched the Brownie camera for just one dollar. Suddenly, anyone could take pictures.

### 2:15 — The yellow empire

[ЭКРАН: жёлтые коробки плёнки по всему миру, карта загорается.]

For most of the 20th century, Kodak dominated. By the 1970s, it was selling the vast majority of film and cameras in the United States.

[ЭКРАН: огромный завод, тысячи сотрудников.]

At its peak, Kodak employed well over a hundred thousand people. "A Kodak moment" became a phrase for any memory worth keeping.

[ЭКРАН: схема бритвы и лезвий: камера дёшево, плёнка дорого.]

And the secret to its profits was simple: film. Kodak didn't make most of its money from cameras. It made money every time you bought film, and every time you printed photos.

Every click was a sale. Remember that. It's the key to the whole story.

### 3:30 — The toaster camera

[ЭКРАН: 1975 год, лаборатория. Молодой инженер Стив Сассон.]

In 1975, a 24-year-old Kodak engineer named Steve Sasson was given a project: see if you can build a camera using a new type of electronic sensor.

[ЭКРАН: громоздкий прибор размером с тостер, детали из разных устройств.]

He built a device about the size of a toaster, weighing around eight pounds. It captured black-and-white images at 0.01 megapixels.

[ЭКРАН: таймер 23 секунды, картинка записывается на кассету.]

It took 23 seconds to record one photo onto a cassette tape. Then you needed a special setup to show it on a TV screen.

It was primitive. But it was the first digital camera. And it worked.

[ЭКРАН: переговорная, руководители скептически смотрят.]

Sasson showed it to Kodak executives. According to him, their reaction was basically: that's cute, but don't tell anyone about it.

### 5:00 — The film trap

[ЭКРАН: весы. С одной стороны горы денег от плёнки, с другой маленькая цифровая камера.]

Why would Kodak bury its own invention?

Because digital photography had one huge problem for Kodak: no film. No film sales. No printing. No chemicals.

[ЭКРАН: руководитель смотрит на график прибыли, потом на камеру, и прячет её.]

Kodak's leaders weren't stupid. Many of them understood digital was coming. Kodak even kept researching digital and built a huge portfolio of patents.

But every step toward digital meant attacking the business that paid everyone's salaries. So Kodak moved slowly. Carefully. Hoping film would last.

[ЭКРАН: часы тикают, годы мелькают: 1980, 1990, 2000.]

It lasted. For a while.

### 6:30 — Too little, too late

[ЭКРАН: 2000-е, цифровые «мыльницы» на полках.]

When digital cameras finally took off in the 2000s, Kodak jumped in. And at first, it did well. For a time, Kodak was one of the top sellers of digital cameras in the US.

[ЭКРАН: цена камеры падает, а маржа исчезает.]

But digital cameras were a cut-throat business with thin profits. Nothing like the money machine of film.

[ЭКРАН: смартфон поглощает камеру.]

And then smartphones came. Suddenly, everyone had a camera in their pocket. And they shared photos online instead of printing them.

Kodak had lost the film business, and the digital camera business was disappearing too.

### 7:45 — Bankruptcy

[ЭКРАН: холодные цвета, январь 2012, печать «CHAPTER 11».]

In January 2012, Eastman Kodak filed for bankruptcy.

[ЭКРАН: аукцион, папки с патентами.]

To survive, it sold off businesses and many of its digital imaging patents. The very technology it had pioneered helped pay off its debts.

[ЭКРАН: маленький современный офис Kodak.]

Kodak still exists today, as a much smaller company focused on printing and other industrial businesses. But the yellow empire is gone.

### 8:45 — The lesson

[ЭКРАН: дверь, за дверью свет. Рука закрывает её.]

Kodak is the classic example of something called the innovator's dilemma. When the new technology threatens your most profitable business, it's incredibly hard to choose the future over the present.

[ЭКРАН: крупно фраза.]

Kodak didn't miss the future. It saw it first. It just couldn't bring itself to walk through the door.
