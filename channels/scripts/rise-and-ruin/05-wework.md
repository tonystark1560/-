# WeWork: How a $47 Billion Startup Fell Apart

**Канал:** Rise & Ruin · **Формат:** 16:9 · **Длина:** около 9–10 минут (примерно 1400 слов)
**Превью:** неоновый офис раскалывается пополам, высокий человек с длинными волосами, крупно «$47B → $0».

## Описание для YouTube

> WeWork rented office space, made it cool, and convinced investors it was worth $47 billion. Then it tried to go public and everyone read the paperwork. The wild story of Adam Neumann, SoftBank, and the startup that "elevated the world's consciousness" all the way to bankruptcy.

## Сценарий

### 0:00 — Intro

[ЭКРАН: стильный офис с неоном, кикером, пивным краном, людьми с ноутбуками.]

Free beer on tap. Neon signs that say "Do what you love." Ping-pong tables. And a CEO who said his company would elevate the world's consciousness.

[ЭКРАН: счётчик оценки растёт до $47,000,000,000.]

In January 2019, WeWork was valued at 47 billion dollars.

[ЭКРАН: стопка бумаг падает на офис, неон гаснет.]

By the end of that year, its CEO was gone, its IPO was dead, and its value had collapsed. This is the story of WeWork.

### 0:45 — The idea

[ЭКРАН: Нью-Йорк, 2010. Пустой этаж в старом здании.]

WeWork was founded in 2010 in New York by Adam Neumann and Miguel McKelvey.

The business model was simple. Lease office space for a long time. Make it beautiful. Split it into desks and small offices. Then rent those out to freelancers and startups for a short time, at a higher price.

[ЭКРАН: схема «long lease → design → short rent», стрелка с долларом.]

It wasn't a new idea. Companies had been doing it for decades. But WeWork didn't describe itself as a real estate company.

It said it was a tech company. A community. A movement.

### 2:00 — The showman

[ЭКРАН: высокий харизматичный основатель на сцене, толпа хлопает.]

Adam Neumann was the perfect founder for that story. Tall, charismatic, full of energy. He talked about changing how the world works and lives.

[ЭКРАН: вечеринки, текила, сёрфинг.]

He was famous for wild parties, tequila shots in the office, and huge ambitions. He talked about WeWork one day housing people, educating kids, and maybe even operating on Mars.

[ЭКРАН: новые бренды: WeLive, WeGrow.]

And for a while, that energy worked. WeWork opened locations in cities around the world at an incredible pace.

### 3:15 — SoftBank's billions

[ЭКРАН: японский инвестор, огромный мешок денег.]

Then came SoftBank and its CEO, Masayoshi Son, one of the boldest investors in the world.

Starting in 2017, SoftBank poured billions of dollars into WeWork. Son reportedly encouraged Neumann to be even crazier, bigger and faster.

[ЭКРАН: оценка растёт ступеньками: $20B → $47B.]

By January 2019, WeWork was valued at 47 billion dollars. More than many real estate companies that actually owned their buildings.

[ЭКРАН: график убытков внизу кадра тоже растёт, только вниз.]

There was just one small detail. WeWork was losing enormous amounts of money. Every year. And the faster it grew, the more it lost.

### 4:45 — The paperwork

[ЭКРАН: август 2019, толстый документ «S-1».]

In August 2019, WeWork filed to go public. To do that, a company has to publish detailed financial documents. And for the first time, everyone could see inside.

[ЭКРАН: лупа над строчками, красные пометки появляются одна за другой.]

It was a disaster. The documents showed WeWork had lost well over a billion dollars the year before.

They also showed strange deals. Neumann owned stakes in buildings that he leased back to WeWork. And the company had paid him millions of dollars for the trademark to the word "We."

[ЭКРАН: частный самолёт, роскошные дома.]

The document even included unusual language about "the energy of We." Investors and the media started asking one question: is this a business, or a cult of personality?

### 6:15 — The fall

[ЭКРАН: кнопка «IPO» трескается.]

Investors weren't buying it. WeWork's target valuation dropped from 47 billion to talk of around 15 billion, then lower.

[ЭКРАН: Нойманн уходит из кадра.]

In September 2019, under pressure from investors, Adam Neumann stepped down as CEO. Days later, the IPO was officially pulled.

[ЭКРАН: SoftBank бросает спасательный круг.]

SoftBank stepped in with a rescue deal that valued WeWork at around 8 billion dollars. And as part of that deal, Neumann himself walked away with a package reportedly worth around a billion dollars.

The company crashed. The founder got rich anyway.

### 7:30 — Bankruptcy

[ЭКРАН: 2020, пустые офисы, маски.]

Then COVID hit. Offices emptied overnight. For a company built on people coming in to work, it was brutal.

[ЭКРАН: «SPAC» с ракетой, которая быстро падает.]

WeWork finally went public in 2021 through a special deal called a SPAC, at a much lower valuation. But the losses kept coming. And it was still stuck with expensive long-term leases.

[ЭКРАН: ноябрь 2023, печать «CHAPTER 11», свет гаснет этаж за этажом.]

In November 2023, WeWork filed for bankruptcy.

### 8:45 — The comeback no one expected

[ЭКРАН: новый логотип-заглушка «Flow», жилые небоскрёбы.]

And Adam Neumann? In 2022, before WeWork's bankruptcy, he started a new real estate company called Flow. It got a huge investment from Andreessen Horowitz, one of Silicon Valley's biggest venture firms.

[ЭКРАН: крупно фраза.]

WeWork is a lesson about what happens when a great story gets ahead of the math. Vibes are not a business model. But apparently, they can still get you funded.
