# The $9 Billion Lie: The Rise & Fall of Theranos

**Канал:** Rise & Ruin · **Формат:** 16:9 · **Длина:** около 10 минут (примерно 1450 слов)
**Превью:** женский силуэт в чёрной водолазке, капля крови на чипе, крупно «$9B LIE», красная трещина.

## Описание для YouTube

> One drop of blood. Hundreds of tests. A $9 billion valuation. And almost none of it was real. How Elizabeth Holmes built Theranos into Silicon Valley's hottest startup, and how two young employees and one reporter brought it all down.

## Сценарий

### 0:00 — The promise

[ЭКРАН: палец, укол, одна капля крови падает в крошечный картридж.]

Imagine walking into a pharmacy. One tiny prick of your finger. One drop of blood. And within hours, you get results for hundreds of medical tests.

No needles. No vials. Cheaper than anything on the market.

[ЭКРАН: обложки бизнес-журналов, вспышки камер.]

That was the promise of Theranos. It made its founder the youngest self-made female billionaire in the world, at least on paper.

[ЭКРАН: картридж трескается, свет гаснет.]

There was just one problem. It didn't work.

### 1:00 — The Stanford dropout

[ЭКРАН: кампус Стэнфорда, 2003 год. Девушка с рюкзаком.]

Elizabeth Holmes grew up idolizing Steve Jobs. She studied chemical engineering at Stanford. And in 2003, at just 19 years old, she dropped out to start a company.

[ЭКРАН: пустой гараж-офис, доска с формулами.]

Her idea came partly from her fear of needles. What if you could run complex blood tests with just a finger prick?

[ЭКРАН: профессор качает головой.]

Some experts told her it wasn't realistic. The amount of blood was too small. The chemistry didn't work that way.

She didn't listen. And at first, that looked like genius.

### 2:30 — Building the legend

[ЭКРАН: Холмс в чёрной водолазке на сцене, низкий голос показан звуковой волной.]

Holmes understood something powerful: in Silicon Valley, the story matters as much as the product.

She wore black turtlenecks like Steve Jobs. She spoke in a deep, calm voice. She rarely blinked in interviews.

[ЭКРАН: стол совета директоров, за ним силуэты пожилых государственных деятелей.]

She built an incredible board of directors: former Secretaries of State George Shultz and Henry Kissinger, future Defense Secretary James Mattis. Powerful names. But almost no one with real expertise in blood testing.

[ЭКРАН: стрелка оценки компании растёт до $9B, деньги падают сверху.]

Investors poured in hundreds of millions. Theranos signed a deal with Walgreens to put its testing centers in stores. By 2014, the company was valued at around 9 billion dollars. Holmes owned about half of it.

[ЭКРАН: обложки журналов: «The next Steve Jobs».]

She was on magazine covers. On stage with presidents. The future of medicine.

### 4:00 — The machine that didn't work

[ЭКРАН: сверкающий аппарат «Edison» в стерильной лаборатории.]

Theranos' device was called the Edison. In demos, it looked amazing.

[ЭКРАН: за шторкой — ряды обычных лабораторных машин других производителей.]

But behind the scenes, it couldn't reliably do what the company promised. So Theranos quietly ran many patient tests on regular, commercially available machines, modified to work with tiny samples. And those results were often wrong.

[ЭКРАН: бланк анализа, красные отметки «ERROR».]

Real patients got real results. Some were told they had conditions they didn't have. Others got false reassurance.

[ЭКРАН: офис разделён стенами, люди не видят друг друга.]

Inside the company, secrecy was extreme. Teams were kept apart so no one could see the full picture. Employees who asked too many questions were pushed out or threatened with lawsuits.

### 5:30 — The whistleblowers

[ЭКРАН: двое молодых сотрудников переглядываются у лабораторного стола.]

But two young employees saw enough.

Tyler Shultz, the grandson of board member George Shultz, and his colleague Erika Cheung noticed that the quality control tests were failing, and results were being manipulated.

[ЭКРАН: Тайлер за ужином с дедом, между ними напряжение.]

Tyler even tried to warn his own grandfather. His grandfather sided with Holmes.

[ЭКРАН: Эрика пишет письмо регулятору.]

Cheung reported the lab's problems to federal regulators. Shultz became a key source for a journalist. Both paid a heavy price. Theranos had lawyers follow them and pressured them to stay quiet.

### 7:00 — The article

[ЭКРАН: редакция газеты, журналист за ноутбуком.]

That journalist was John Carreyrou of the Wall Street Journal.

[ЭКРАН: заголовок газеты влетает в кадр, 2015.]

In October 2015, he published an investigation showing that Theranos' technology didn't work the way it claimed, and that most tests were done on other machines.

[ЭКРАН: домино падает: регуляторы, Walgreens, инвесторы.]

Theranos called it false. But the dominoes started falling. Regulators inspected the lab and found serious problems. Walgreens cut ties. Tens of thousands of test results were voided.

[ЭКРАН: оценка компании падает с $9B до нуля.]

In 2018, the SEC charged Holmes with massive fraud. Later that year, Theranos was dissolved. Its value went from 9 billion dollars to essentially zero.

### 8:15 — The trial

[ЭКРАН: зал суда, Холмс уже не в водолазке, а в деловом костюме.]

In 2022, a jury found Elizabeth Holmes guilty of defrauding investors.

[ЭКРАН: «11 YEARS 3 MONTHS» жёлтым.]

She was sentenced to more than 11 years in federal prison, and reported to prison in 2023. Her former business partner and number two, Sunny Balwani, was also convicted and sentenced to almost 13 years.

### 9:15 — The lesson

[ЭКРАН: пустая лаборатория, свет гаснет одна лампа за другой.]

How did so many smart, powerful people believe it?

Because they wanted to. The story was perfect: a young genius, a world-changing idea, a hero founder. Asking hard questions felt like being on the wrong side of history.

[ЭКРАН: крупно фраза.]

Theranos is a reminder that confidence is not proof. And that sometimes, the bravest people in the room are the ones who ask: "Can you show me?"
