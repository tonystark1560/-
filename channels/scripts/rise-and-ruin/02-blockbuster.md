# Blockbuster Laughed at Netflix. Then This Happened.

**Канал:** Rise & Ruin · **Формат:** 16:9 · **Длина:** около 10 минут (примерно 1450 слов)
**Превью:** синяя вывеска Blockbuster треснула, рядом маленький красный конверт, крупно «$50M?», человек в костюме смеётся.

## Описание для YouTube

> In 2000, Netflix offered to sell itself to Blockbuster for $50 million. Blockbuster said no. Ten years later, Blockbuster was bankrupt and Netflix was on its way to becoming one of the most valuable media companies on Earth. This is the full story.

## Сценарий

### 0:00 — The meeting

[ЭКРАН: 2000 год, Даллас. Переговорная на высоком этаже. За столом трое молодых основателей и CEO в дорогом костюме.]

Dallas, Texas. The year 2000.

Three people from a small, money-losing startup walk into the headquarters of the biggest video rental company in the world. Their pitch: buy us for 50 million dollars, and we'll run your online business.

[ЭКРАН: конверт «$50,000,000» скользит по столу.]

According to Netflix co-founder Marc Randolph, the Blockbuster executives listened politely… and then they could barely keep from laughing.

[ЭКРАН: CEO смеётся, конверт улетает обратно.]

The startup was Netflix. And that meeting might be one of the most expensive "no"s in business history.

### 1:00 — Friday night empire

[ЭКРАН: тёплые цвета. Улица пригорода, синие и жёлтые вывески горят в сумерках.]

To understand how big that mistake was, you have to understand how big Blockbuster was.

The first Blockbuster opened in Dallas in 1985. Within a few years, under businessman Wayne Huizenga, it was opening stores at a crazy pace. Sometimes more than one a day.

[ЭКРАН: карта США заполняется синими точками.]

By the mid-2000s, Blockbuster had around nine thousand stores around the world and tens of thousands of employees.

[ЭКРАН: семья в магазине, ребёнок держит кассету, стена новинок.]

Friday night meant one thing for millions of families: drive to Blockbuster, walk the aisles, fight over what to watch, grab popcorn at the counter.

It wasn't just a store. It was a ritual. And Blockbuster owned it.

### 2:45 — The late fee problem

[ЭКРАН: кассета под кроватью, календарь с днями просрочки.]

But there was one part of the ritual that everyone hated.

Late fees.

You forgot to return a movie? Pay up. Forgot for a week? Pay a lot.

[ЭКРАН: стопка чеков растёт, счётчик долларов.]

And here's the dirty secret: late fees weren't a side hustle for Blockbuster. They were a huge part of the business. By some estimates, hundreds of millions of dollars a year.

[ЭКРАН: недовольные лица покупателей на кассе.]

Blockbuster was making a fortune by annoying its own customers. And whenever a company does that, it's leaving the door open for someone else.

### 4:00 — A tiny DVD startup

[ЭКРАН: тесный офис, коробки с DVD, красные конверты.]

Netflix was founded in 1997 by Reed Hastings and Marc Randolph. The famous legend says Hastings started it after getting a forty-dollar late fee for a movie. Randolph has said the real story is less dramatic. But either way, the idea was clear.

[ЭКРАН: схема: сайт → почтовый ящик → конверт → обратно.]

Rent DVDs online. Get them in the mail. Keep them as long as you want. Monthly subscription. No late fees. Ever.

[ЭКРАН: почтальон с красными конвертами.]

In the beginning, Netflix was tiny and losing money. That's why, in 2000, after the dot-com crash, its founders were willing to sell. And that's why Blockbuster thought it was a joke.

Why would a giant with thousands of stores buy a company that mails DVDs?

### 5:30 — Blockbuster fights back

[ЭКРАН: график: красная линия Netflix медленно ползёт вверх.]

But Netflix didn't go away. It kept growing. Subscribers loved it.

[ЭКРАН: в офисе Blockbuster срочное совещание.]

And to be fair, Blockbuster eventually saw the threat. Under CEO John Antioco, the company launched its own online rental service in 2004. It also got rid of late fees, at a huge cost.

[ЭКРАН: реклама «Total Access»: верни диск в магазин, получи новый сразу.]

Then it launched something called Total Access. You could rent online, and return DVDs to a store for a free movie on the spot. It was actually great. Netflix executives later admitted it scared them.

[ЭКРАН: кривые подписчиков сближаются.]

For a moment, it looked like Blockbuster might win.

### 7:00 — The wrong turn

[ЭКРАН: зал заседаний совета директоров, напряжение.]

But fighting back was expensive. Investors were unhappy. And inside the company, there was a power struggle.

In 2007, after clashing with activist investor Carl Icahn over strategy and pay, Antioco left. The new CEO, Jim Keyes, believed the future was still in physical stores.

[ЭКРАН: стрелка разворачивается от «online» обратно к «stores».]

Blockbuster pulled back on its online push and refocused on retail.

[ЭКРАН: 2007 год. На экране ноутбука кнопка «Watch Now».]

The same year, Netflix launched streaming.

### 8:15 — Collapse

[ЭКРАН: холодные цвета. Магазины гаснут один за другим.]

From there, it went fast. Streaming grew. Redbox kiosks offered DVDs for a dollar. And the 2008 financial crisis crushed a company already drowning in debt.

[ЭКРАН: печать «CHAPTER 11», 2010.]

In September 2010, Blockbuster filed for bankruptcy. The next year, its remaining business was bought at auction by Dish Network. By early 2014, the last company-owned stores were closed.

[ЭКРАН: график Netflix уходит за пределы экрана вверх.]

Netflix? It became one of the most valuable entertainment companies in the world, worth hundreds of billions of dollars. Roughly a thousand times more than the price Blockbuster laughed at.

### 9:15 — The last store

[ЭКРАН: ночь, маленький город, единственная горящая вывеска Blockbuster.]

Today, there's one Blockbuster left on Earth. In Bend, Oregon. It's a franchise store that refused to close, and it's become a tourist attraction.

[ЭКРАН: люди фотографируются у вывески.]

People fly in just to rent a movie there. Not because they need to. Because they remember.

[ЭКРАН: крупно фраза.]

Blockbuster didn't fail because it didn't see the future. It saw it, fought for it, and then turned back.
