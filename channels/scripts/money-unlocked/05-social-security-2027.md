## Сценарий

### 0:00 — The raise

[ЭКРАН: счётчик быстро бежит до 76 000 000, маскот хлопает.]

About 76 million Americans just got a raise.

[ЭКРАН: карточка «COLA 2027: +3.6%».]

On October 14, 2026, the Social Security Administration announced that Social Security and Supplemental Security Income payments will increase by 3.6 percent in 2027.

[ЭКРАН: столбики 2024–2027: 3.2%, 2.5%, 2.8%, 3.6%.]

That's the biggest increase in several years. In 2024, the increase was 3.2 percent. In 2025, it was 2.5 percent. In 2026, 2.8 percent. And now, 3.6.

[ЭКРАН: маскот с табличкой «let's unlock it».]

So what does this mean in real dollars? Why does it happen? And is it actually as good as it sounds? Let's unlock it.

### 0:50 — What is a COLA?

[ЭКРАН: карточка «COLA = Cost-Of-Living Adjustment».]

The increase is called a COLA, which stands for cost-of-living adjustment.

[ЭКРАН: тележка продуктов дорожает: $100 → $103.60.]

The idea is simple. When prices go up, the same check buys less. So Social Security benefits are adjusted each year to help them keep up with inflation.

[ЭКРАН: 1975 год, документ с печатью.]

Automatic yearly adjustments have been in place since 1975. Before that, Congress had to vote on increases. Now it happens on its own, based on a formula.

[ЭКРАН: две группы: пенсионеры и получатели SSI, люди с инвалидностью.]

The COLA applies to retirement benefits, disability benefits, survivor benefits and Supplemental Security Income, or SSI, which supports people with very low income who are elderly, blind or disabled.

[ЭКРАН: столбик 2023 года огромный: «8.7%».]

The biggest COLA in recent memory came in 2023, when benefits jumped 8.7 percent after inflation spiked. That was the largest increase in about four decades. Since then, the increases have been smaller as inflation cooled, which is why 3.6 percent stands out now. It tells you that prices were still rising faster this year than many people hoped.

### 2:00 — How it's calculated

[ЭКРАН: график индекса цен, три месяца подсвечиваются: July, August, September.]

So where does 3.6 percent come from? It's based on a measure of inflation called the CPI-W: the Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers.

[ЭКРАН: формула: «average CPI-W (Jul–Sep 2026) vs (Jul–Sep 2025)».]

The government takes the average CPI-W for July, August and September of this year, compares it to the same three months last year, and the percentage increase becomes the COLA, rounded to the nearest tenth of a percent.

[ЭКРАН: корзина работающего человека против корзины пенсионера: бензин vs лекарства.]

Here's a detail many people don't know: the CPI-W tracks the spending of working people, not retirees. Older Americans tend to spend more on things like healthcare and housing. That's why some advocacy groups have long argued for a different index designed for seniors. So far, the formula hasn't changed.

### 3:15 — How much more you'll get

[ЭКРАН: конверт с чеком, «+$75» выпрыгивает.]

So how much more money is this? According to the Social Security Administration, the average retirement benefit will increase by about $75 per month starting in January 2027.

[ЭКРАН: таблица примеров: $1,500 → +$54, $2,000 → +$72, $3,000 → +$108.]

You can estimate yours easily. Take your current monthly benefit and multiply it by 0.036. For example, if you get $2,000 a month, that's about $72 more. If you get $1,500, about $54 more.

[ЭКРАН: календарь: январь — пенсии, конец декабря — SSI.]

Retirement and disability benefits go up with the January 2027 payments. SSI recipients typically see the increase a bit earlier, at the very end of December.

[ЭКРАН: аккаунт «my Social Security» на ноутбуке.]

Your exact new amount will be in your COLA notice, which you can usually see online in your "my Social Security" account, or get by mail, before the increase starts.

### 4:30 — What changes for workers

[ЭКРАН: работающий человек, зарплатный лист, строка «Social Security tax».]

The COLA isn't just for people getting benefits. Some numbers change for workers too.

[ЭКРАН: карточка «Taxable max: $184,500 → $192,300».]

The biggest one: the maximum amount of income subject to Social Security tax. In 2027, it goes up from $184,500 to $192,300. If you earn more than that, you stop paying Social Security tax on the income above it for the year.

[ЭКРАН: маскот работает, будучи пенсионером, лимит заработка.]

There are also limits on how much you can earn if you claim benefits before your full retirement age and keep working. Those limits usually rise each year too. If you're in that situation, check the new numbers on ssa.gov.

### 5:30 — The catch

[ЭКРАН: весы: «+3.6%» против «rent, food, medicine».]

Now the catch. A raise that matches inflation doesn't make you richer. It mostly keeps you even.

[ЭКРАН: рост цен на аренду и медицину обгоняет +3.6%.]

And if your personal costs, like rent, healthcare or insurance, went up faster than 3.6 percent, you may actually feel like you're falling behind, even with the increase.

[ЭКРАН: «Medicare Part B premium» съедает кусок прибавки.]

There's another thing to watch: Medicare premiums. For many retirees, Medicare Part B premiums are taken directly out of their Social Security check. If premiums go up, part of your COLA gets eaten before it ever reaches you. The 2027 premiums are usually announced in the fall, so watch for that.

[ЭКРАН: налоговая форма, часть пенсии облагается налогом.]

And depending on your total income, part of your Social Security may be taxable. A bigger benefit can sometimes mean a slightly bigger tax bill.

[ЭКРАН: «The bigger worry», песочные часы с надписью «trust fund».]

There's also a bigger, long-term issue that's worth knowing about. Social Security's main trust fund has been projected to run short in the 2030s, according to the program's own trustees reports. If Congress doesn't act before then, benefits could be automatically cut to match the money coming in from payroll taxes.

[ЭКРАН: «not zero», большая часть столбика остаётся.]

That doesn't mean Social Security disappears. Payroll taxes would still cover most benefits. But it does mean changes are likely at some point, like higher taxes, adjusted benefits or a later retirement age, or some mix of those. That's a decision for Congress, and it's worth paying attention to, especially if you're planning your retirement.

### 6:45 — What to do now

[ЭКРАН: чек-лист.]

So what should you do?

[ЭКРАН: 1: войти в «my Social Security».]

If you get benefits: log in to your "my Social Security" account and check your COLA notice when it's available, so you know your exact new amount.

[ЭКРАН: 2: бюджет с новой суммой.]

Update your budget. Don't just let the extra $50 or $100 disappear. Decide where it goes, whether that's covering higher bills, building a small cushion, or paying down debt.

[ЭКРАН: 3: пожилой родственник, внук помогает с телефоном.]

If you have older parents or grandparents, share this with them, and maybe help them check their account. A lot of people miss these notices.

[ЭКРАН: 4: молодой работник смотрит на свою выписку Social Security.]

And if you're still working, it's a good moment to check your own Social Security statement online. It shows your earnings history and estimated future benefits. Mistakes in your earnings record can cost you later, so it's worth a look.

### 7:45 — Recap

[ЭКРАН: «Quick recap», карточки складываются в стопку.]

Quick recap. Social Security and SSI benefits rise 3.6 percent in 2027, the biggest increase in several years. That's about $75 more per month for the average retiree, starting in January. The taxable maximum for workers rises to $192,300. And remember the catch: the COLA is meant to keep up with prices, and Medicare premiums and taxes can take a bite.
