## Сценарий

### 0:00 — The scroll-to-cart pipeline

[ЭКРАН: телефон, лента новостей с тревожными заголовками, палец листает.]

You're scrolling the news. Layoffs. Prices going up. Something scary happening somewhere in the world. Your chest feels tight.

[ЭКРАН: палец переходит в приложение магазина, корзина наполняется.]

And then, almost without thinking, you're in a shopping app. New shoes. A gadget. Takeout. Five minutes later, you've spent a hundred dollars, and you feel a little better. For about an hour.

[ЭКРАН: маскот с табличкой «let's unlock it».]

There's a name for that: doom spending. And it might be one of the biggest hidden reasons people feel broke. Let's unlock it.

### 0:50 — What is doom spending?

[ЭКРАН: определение на карточке.]

Doom spending is spending money, often on non-essentials, because you feel anxious or hopeless about the future.

[ЭКРАН: пузырь мысли: «Why save? Everything's falling apart anyway».]

The logic goes something like this: "If the economy is a mess, if I'll never afford a house anyway, if the world is falling apart, I might as well enjoy my money now."

[ЭКРАН: молодые люди с телефонами, графики опросов.]

The term took off with younger Americans, especially Gen Z and millennials. Many feel locked out of traditional milestones like buying a home, and that makes saving feel pointless.

[ЭКРАН: родственники трендов: «soft saving», «revenge spending», «treat culture».]

It's related to other trends you might have heard of, like "treat culture," buying yourself small rewards to get through the day, and "soft saving," prioritizing enjoying life now over saving aggressively for later.

[ЭКРАН: весы: «enjoy now» и «future me».]

Now, enjoying your life isn't wrong. The problem is when spending becomes your main way to cope with stress, and quietly eats the money you'll need later.

### 2:00 — Why your brain does it

[ЭКРАН: мультяшный мозг, зоны загораются.]

So why does this happen? It's not a lack of willpower. It's how our brains are wired.

[ЭКРАН: стрелка «stress» → «buy» → вспышка «dopamine».]

When we're stressed, our brain looks for quick relief. Buying something gives a little hit of reward. The anticipation, the "order confirmed" email, the package arriving. It feels good, fast.

[ЭКРАН: рука держит руль: «control».]

Shopping also gives a feeling of control. You can't fix the economy. But you can choose a new jacket. In a world that feels chaotic, that choice feels powerful.

[ЭКРАН: часы: «now» огромные, «future» маленькие.]

And our brains naturally value now over later. A reward today feels much more real than money in a savings account years from now. Psychologists call this "present bias."

[ЭКРАН: телефон превращается в магазин, который всегда открыт.]

Add a phone that's a store open 24/7, with one-click checkout and buy now, pay later buttons everywhere, and you have the perfect storm.

### 3:30 — Signs you're doing it

[ЭКРАН: чек-лист с отметками.]

How do you know if this is you? Here are some signs.

You shop more after reading bad news or after a hard day.

You buy things and forget about them. Packages pile up unopened.

You feel a rush when ordering, and a little guilt or emptiness afterward.

You say "I deserve this" a lot, and your savings never seem to grow.

[ЭКРАН: маскот смотрит в выписку, лупа над датами.]

Quick test: open your bank app and look at last month's purchases. Next to each non-essential one, try to remember how you felt that day. If you see a lot of "stressed," "bored" or "anxious," you've found your pattern.

### 4:45 — What it really costs

[ЭКРАН: маленькие покупки складываются в большой столб.]

Let's talk about what doom spending really costs. Say you spend an extra $150 a month on stress purchases. That doesn't sound huge.

[ЭКРАН: калькулятор: $150 × 12 = $1,800.]

But that's $1,800 a year. Over five years, that's $9,000. Enough for a solid emergency fund, or a big chunk of a car, or a serious dent in credit card debt.

[ЭКРАН: кредитная карта, проценты растут как снежный ком.]

And if those purchases go on a credit card you don't pay off in full, the interest can make them far more expensive than the price tag.

[ЭКРАН: замкнутый круг: «stress → spend → less money → more stress».]

Here's the cruel part: doom spending can create the very money stress that started it. You spend because you feel anxious. Then you have less money, which makes you more anxious. And around it goes.

### 5:45 — Six ways to stop

[ЭКРАН: шесть карточек выстраиваются в сетку.]

The good news: you can break the cycle without becoming miserable. Here are six ways.

[ЭКРАН: 1: таймер 48 часов над корзиной.]

One: the 48-hour rule. Put it in the cart, then wait two days. If you still want it and can afford it, fine. Most of the time, the urge fades.

[ЭКРАН: 2: удаление сохранённой карты в браузере.]

Two: add friction. Delete saved cards from shopping sites. Remove shopping apps from your home screen. Turn off sale notifications. Every extra step gives your brain a second to think.

[ЭКРАН: 3: монетка летит в сейф каждый раз при «желании купить».]

Three: redirect the urge. Every time you feel like stress shopping, move a small amount, even $5, into savings instead. You still get the feeling of "doing something," but it builds your future.

[ЭКРАН: 4: прогулка, звонок другу, спортзал, музыка.]

Four: find a cheaper comfort. A walk. Calling a friend. Exercise. A favorite show you already pay for. Your brain wants relief, not necessarily a package.

[ЭКРАН: 5: телефон с ограничением на новостные приложения.]

Five: limit doom scrolling. Set a time limit on news and social apps, especially at night. Less doom in, less spending out.

[ЭКРАН: 6: конверт «Fun money» с фиксированной суммой.]

And six: give yourself a guilt-free fun budget. Decide on a monthly amount just for treats. When it's gone, it's gone. That way, you can enjoy your money without the spiral.

[ЭКРАН: «Plan for the moment», маскот пишет на стикере.]

One more powerful trick: plan for the moment before it happens. Psychologists call these "if-then" plans. For example: "If I feel the urge to shop after reading the news, then I'll put my phone down and go for a ten-minute walk." Or: "If I'm about to order delivery because I'm stressed, then I'll make the easy meal I keep in the freezer."

[ЭКРАН: стикер на холодильнике, маскот следует плану.]

It sounds simple, but deciding in advance takes the decision away from your stressed-out brain in the moment. And that's exactly when doom spending wins.

[ЭКРАН: «Quick recap», карточки складываются в стопку.]

Quick recap. Doom spending is buying to cope with anxiety about the future. It feels good fast because of reward, control and present bias. It can quietly cost thousands a year and make your stress worse. The fix: add friction, wait 48 hours, redirect the urge into savings, find cheaper comforts, limit doom scrolling and give yourself a guilt-free fun budget.

### 7:45 — The bigger picture

[ЭКРАН: маскот под тучей открывает зонт, туча рассеивается.]

Feeling anxious about the future makes total sense right now. A lot of people are struggling. But spending your way through the anxiety usually makes the future harder, not easier.

[ЭКРАН: маскот кладёт монетку в копилку, копилка светится.]

The most powerful thing you can do when the world feels out of control is to build a little cushion that gives you options. Even a small emergency fund can make bad news feel a lot less scary.
