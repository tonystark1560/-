## Сценарий

### 0:00 — The myth

[ЭКРАН: цепь с замком на дипломе, маскот тянет её.]

Everyone says student loans never go away. That they'll follow you forever, even into retirement.

[ЭКРАН: надпись «not quite true», ключ появляется рядом с замком.]

That's not quite true. But it's also not easy. And the rules have changed a lot recently.

[ЭКРАН: маскот с табличкой «let's unlock it».]

So in this video: what changed, what options still exist, and the truth about getting rid of student loans in bankruptcy. Let's unlock it.

### 0:50 — The big picture

[ЭКРАН: счётчик: десятки миллионов заёмщиков, более $1.5 трлн долга.]

Student debt is huge in America. More than 40 million people have federal student loans, and together they owe more than one and a half trillion dollars.

[ЭКРАН: два типа кредитов: «Federal» и «Private».]

First, an important difference. Federal student loans come from the government. Private student loans come from banks and other lenders. They follow very different rules. Most of the options in this video apply to federal loans. Private loans usually have far fewer protections.

[ЭКРАН: «know your loans», маскот смотрит на studentaid.gov.]

If you're not sure which kind you have, you can see your federal loans by logging in at studentaid.gov. Anything that doesn't show up there is probably private.

### 2:00 — What changed

[ЭКРАН: таймлайн: 2020 пауза платежей → 2023 возобновление → 2025 новый закон → 2026 изменения вступают в силу.]

The last few years have been a roller coaster.

[ЭКРАН: 2020: кнопка «pause» на платежах.]

During the pandemic, federal student loan payments were paused for more than three years. Then payments restarted.

[ЭКРАН: 2023: суд, отменённая массовая программа прощения.]

A huge plan to cancel student debt for millions of borrowers was struck down by the Supreme Court in 2023. Other repayment plans created afterward faced legal challenges too.

[ЭКРАН: 2025: Конгресс, большая папка с законом.]

Then, in 2025, Congress passed a major law that rewrote a lot of the federal student loan system. Among the changes: a new repayment plan for new borrowers, new limits on how much some graduate students and parents can borrow, and the phase-out of some older programs. Many of those changes started rolling out in July 2026.

[ЭКРАН: маскот в замешательстве среди стрелок.]

If you feel confused, you're not alone. The most important thing is to check your own loans and your own plan, because what applies to you depends on when you borrowed and what kind of loans you have.

### 3:30 — Repayment options

[ЭКРАН: две дорожки: «standard plan» и «income-driven plan».]

So what are your options for paying? In general, there are two big types of repayment plans.

[ЭКРАН: стандартный план: одинаковые платежи на 10 лет.]

Fixed plans, where you pay the same amount each month over a set number of years. Simple, and you usually pay less interest overall.

[ЭКРАН: план, зависящий от дохода: платёж растёт и падает вместе с зарплатой.]

And income-driven plans, where your monthly payment is based on your income and family size. If your income is low, your payment can be low. In some cases, very low. And after many years of payments, any remaining balance may be forgiven.

[ЭКРАН: калькулятор «Loan Simulator» на studentaid.gov.]

The best tool to compare plans is the Loan Simulator on studentaid.gov. You put in your loans and income, and it shows your estimated payments under each plan you qualify for.

[ЭКРАН: красный знак: «default».]

The worst thing you can do is ignore your loans. Federal student loans that go into default can lead to wage garnishment, seized tax refunds, and even part of your Social Security being taken. If you can't afford your payments, contact your loan servicer before you fall behind.

### 4:45 — Forgiveness that still exists

[ЭКРАН: три двери с табличками.]

What about forgiveness? Despite all the changes, some well-established programs still exist. Let's look at three.

[ЭКРАН: дверь 1: «Public Service Loan Forgiveness», учитель, медсестра, госслужащий.]

Public Service Loan Forgiveness, or PSLF. If you work full-time for the government or a qualifying nonprofit and make 120 qualifying payments, which is 10 years, the rest of your federal Direct Loans can be forgiven, tax-free.

[ЭКРАН: дверь 2: «Disability discharge», инвалидное кресло, документ.]

Total and permanent disability discharge, for borrowers who have a qualifying disability.

[ЭКРАН: дверь 3: «School closure / fraud», закрытый колледж.]

And discharges if your school closed while you were enrolled, or if it misled you in certain ways.

[ЭКРАН: предупреждение: «Beware of scams», мошенник с телефоном.]

And a warning: scammers love student loan confusion. If a company calls promising to wipe out your loans for an upfront fee, be very careful. You can apply for federal programs yourself, for free, through studentaid.gov.

### 5:45 — Bankruptcy: the truth

[ЭКРАН: судебный молоток, папка «bankruptcy».]

Now, bankruptcy. For most debts, like credit cards or medical bills, bankruptcy can wipe them out. Student loans are different.

[ЭКРАН: карточка «undue hardship».]

To discharge student loans in bankruptcy, you have to prove "undue hardship," meaning that repaying would make it impossible for you to keep a minimal standard of living, and that your situation is likely to last. Historically, courts set that bar very high, and many people never even tried.

[ЭКРАН: 2022 год, упрощённая форма-анкета.]

But in November 2022, the Department of Justice and the Department of Education introduced a simpler process for federal student loans. Borrowers fill out a standard form, called an attestation, showing their income, expenses and circumstances. Government lawyers then review it and can agree not to fight the discharge.

[ЭКРАН: люди проходят через ворота, полоска прогресса.]

Since then, many borrowers who used this process have had their federal student loans fully or partly discharged. It's still not automatic, and it's not for everyone. But it's more realistic than it used to be.

[ЭКРАН: маскот звонит юристу, иконка весов правосудия.]

Bankruptcy has serious consequences for your credit and finances. So if you're thinking about it, talk to a bankruptcy attorney. Some offer free consultations, and legal aid organizations can help if money is tight.

[ЭКРАН: «Private loans», отдельная карточка, банк.]

What if you have private student loans? Your options are more limited, but you still have some.

[ЭКРАН: телефонная трубка, кредитор, «hardship program».]

Call your lender and ask about hardship options. Some private lenders offer temporary reduced payments or forbearance if you're struggling.

[ЭКРАН: рефинансирование, ставка уменьшается.]

If your credit and income have improved since you borrowed, refinancing might get you a lower interest rate. But be careful: never refinance federal loans into a private loan without understanding what you lose, because you give up federal protections like income-driven plans and forgiveness programs.

[ЭКРАН: часы, срок давности.]

And in bankruptcy, private student loans generally face the same "undue hardship" standard, without the federal attestation process. That's another reason to talk to an attorney if you're considering it.

[ЭКРАН: «Quick recap», карточки складываются в стопку.]

Quick recap. Know whether your loans are federal or private. Rules changed a lot recently, so check studentaid.gov. Income-driven plans can lower payments. PSLF and other discharge programs still exist. Bankruptcy is hard, but a simpler process for federal loans has made it more realistic for some people.

### 7:15 — What to do now

[ЭКРАН: чек-лист из четырёх пунктов.]

So, here's your action plan.

One: log in to studentaid.gov and see exactly what loans you have, who your servicer is, and what plan you're on.

Two: use the Loan Simulator to check if a different repayment plan would work better for you.

Three: if you work in public service, look into PSLF right away and make sure your employment is certified.

And four: if you're truly drowning and can't see a way out, talk to a bankruptcy attorney about whether the attestation process could apply to you.

[ЭКРАН: ключ поворачивается в замке, цепь ослабевает.]

Student loans are hard to escape. But "never" isn't the right word anymore.
