## Сценарий

### 0:00 — Four easy payments

[ЭКРАН: страница оплаты в интернет-магазине, кнопка «4 payments of $25».]

You're checking out online. The total is $100. And right under it, there's a friendly little button: "Pay in 4 interest-free payments of $25."

[ЭКРАН: маскот нажимает кнопку, улыбается.]

It feels like a hack. Free money. Why pay $100 today when you can pay $25?

[ЭКРАН: с карточки слетает милая маска, под ней надпись «DEBT».]

But buy now, pay later, or BNPL, is still debt. Just wearing a cute outfit. And for millions of people, it's quietly turning into a problem. Let's unlock the math.

### 0:50 — How BNPL works

[ЭКРАН: схема: покупатель → BNPL-компания платит магазину → покупатель возвращает частями.]

Here's how the most common type works. When you choose BNPL at checkout, a BNPL company pays the store for your purchase. Then you pay that company back, usually in four payments every two weeks. The first payment is often due right away.

[ЭКРАН: календарь с четырьмя точками через каждые 2 недели.]

The classic "Pay in 4" plans often charge no interest if you pay on time.

[ЭКРАН: второй тип: долгий план на 12 месяцев с процентом.]

But there are also longer plans, for bigger purchases, that can last for months or years. Those often do charge interest, sometimes at rates similar to credit cards.

[ЭКРАН: BNPL встроен в магазины, в приложения, даже в доставку еды.]

BNPL started with clothes and electronics. Now you can use it for almost anything: furniture, travel, concert tickets, even groceries and food delivery.

### 2:00 — How they make money

[ЭКРАН: маскот с лупой: «If it's free, who pays?»]

If it's interest-free, how do these companies make money? Mostly in three ways.

[ЭКРАН: стрелка от магазина к BNPL-компании: «merchant fee».]

One: stores pay them. Merchants pay the BNPL company a fee on each sale, often higher than what they pay for credit cards. Why would stores agree? Because studies and industry data suggest people buy more, and spend more per order, when BNPL is available.

[ЭКРАН: штраф «LATE FEE» падает на маскота.]

Two: late fees. If you miss a payment, many providers charge a fee.

[ЭКРАН: долгий план с процентами, график процентов.]

And three: interest on the longer plans.

[ЭКРАН: крупно фраза.]

So BNPL works best for the companies when you spend more than you planned. Keep that in mind.

### 3:15 — The stacking trap

[ЭКРАН: одна карточка платежа, потом две, пять, восемь — стопка до потолка.]

The real danger isn't one BNPL purchase. It's stacking.

Sneakers on one plan. Headphones on another. A jacket on a third. Groceries on a fourth. Each one feels small. "It's just $20."

[ЭКРАН: календарь месяца, весь в красных точках, маскот в панике.]

Suddenly you've got six different payment plans, with twelve due dates a month, spread across different apps. And you have no idea what you actually owe in total.

[ЭКРАН: калькулятор: 6 планов × ~$30 каждые 2 недели = $360+ в месяц.]

Let's do the math. Six plans with around $30 due every two weeks. That's roughly $360 a month in payments. That might be more than your car payment, and you don't even have a car to show for it.

[ЭКРАН: «phantom debt», полупрозрачный призрак-долг.]

Some experts call this "phantom debt," because it often doesn't show up in the places people normally look, like a credit card statement. That makes it easy to lose track.

### 4:45 — Missed payments

[ЭКРАН: автосписание с пустого счёта, красный крестик.]

What happens if you miss a payment?

[ЭКРАН: «LATE FEE» и «overdraft fee» вместе.]

First, the late fee. And if the BNPL payment is pulled automatically from your bank account when there isn't enough money, your bank might charge you an overdraft fee too. Two fees for one small purchase.

[ЭКРАН: замок на BNPL-аккаунте.]

Your BNPL account might be frozen, so you can't use it again.

[ЭКРАН: стрелка к кредитному рейтингу, рейтинг падает.]

And increasingly, BNPL can affect your credit. Some providers report payments to credit bureaus, and credit scoring companies have been working on ways to include BNPL in credit scores. If you fall far behind, the debt can even go to collections, which can seriously hurt your credit.

### 5:45 — Why it's so tempting

[ЭКРАН: мозг: «$100» выглядит страшно, «$25» — нет.]

Why does BNPL feel so easy? Because it plays tricks on our brains.

Breaking a price into small pieces makes it feel smaller. "$25" doesn't hurt like "$100," even though you'll pay the full $100 either way.

[ЭКРАН: одна кнопка, без заполнения анкет.]

There's almost no friction. No long application. An instant decision. Sometimes just one click.

[ЭКРАН: «Future you will pay», рядом маскот-будущий, грустный.]

And it pushes the pain to the future. Today's you gets the item. Future you gets the bill.

[ЭКРАН: «Returns & disputes», коробка возврата, вопросительный знак.]

There's also a hidden headache: returns. If you return something bought with BNPL, the refund usually has to go through both the store and the BNPL company. While that's being sorted out, you may still be expected to keep making payments. If you stop paying because "I returned it," you can get hit with late fees.

[ЭКРАН: сравнение защиты: кредитная карта со щитом и BNPL с маленьким щитком.]

And while credit cards have long-established legal protections for disputes and fraud, protections for BNPL users have been less clear and have changed over time. So if something goes wrong, sorting it out can be more complicated.

[ЭКРАН: молодые люди, данные опросов.]

Surveys have found that a significant share of BNPL users have paid late at least once. And BNPL is especially popular with younger shoppers, who may have less of a financial cushion when something goes wrong.

### 6:45 — When it's OK

[ЭКРАН: зелёная карточка «When BNPL can work».]

To be fair, BNPL isn't always bad. It can make sense if three things are true.

[ЭКРАН: три галочки появляются по очереди.]

One: you could afford to pay the full price today, and you're just spreading it out for convenience.

Two: it's truly interest-free and fee-free, and you'll pay on time. Set up reminders.

Three: you only have one or two plans at a time, not a stack.

[ЭКРАН: сравнение с кредиткой: BNPL без процентов против кредитки с высоким процентом.]

In that case, a 0% BNPL plan can actually be cheaper than putting the purchase on a credit card you won't pay off in full.

[ЭКРАН: красная карточка: «groceries / bills / emergencies».]

But if you're using BNPL for groceries, bills or everyday basics because you can't cover them otherwise, that's a warning sign that your budget needs attention. That's when it's time to step back.

### 7:45 — The rule

[ЭКРАН: маскот собирает все планы BNPL в одну таблицу.]

If you already have several BNPL plans, here's a simple first step: list them all in one place. Amounts, due dates, which app. Then stop adding new ones until the list is paid off.

[ЭКРАН: календарь с напоминаниями, автосписание с карты, на которой точно есть деньги.]

Set calendar reminders a couple of days before each due date. Make sure the account linked for automatic payments always has enough money in it. And if you know you're going to miss a payment, contact the company before the due date. Some will work with you, and it's always better than silence.

[ЭКРАН: крупно фраза, слово «one» в плашке.]

[ЭКРАН: «Quick recap», карточки складываются в стопку.]

Quick recap. BNPL splits a purchase into payments, often interest-free if you pay on time. Companies make money from store fees, late fees and interest on longer plans. The real danger is stacking many plans until you lose track. Missed payments can mean fees, overdrafts and credit damage. It can be fine for planned purchases you could already afford, but it's a warning sign if you need it for basics.

And remember this rule of thumb: if you can't afford it in one payment, you probably can't afford it in four.
