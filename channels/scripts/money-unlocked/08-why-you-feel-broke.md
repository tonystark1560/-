## Сценарий

### 0:00 — The paycheck paradox

[ЭКРАН: зарплата растёт стрелкой вверх, а лицо маскота грустнеет.]

Your paycheck went up over the past few years. Maybe a raise, maybe a new job. So why do you feel poorer than before?

[ЭКРАН: чек из магазина, глаза маскота расширяются.]

You check out at the grocery store and wince. You look at rent prices and laugh, because what else can you do. You open your bank app and wonder where it all went.

[ЭКРАН: маскот с табличкой «let's unlock it».]

If that sounds familiar, you're definitely not alone. Let's unlock why so many Americans feel broke, even when the numbers say they're not, and what to do about it.

### 0:50 — The numbers

[ЭКРАН: индикатор настроения уходит в красную зону, подпись «NY Fed survey».]

First, the mood. Recent surveys from the New York Federal Reserve show Americans have become more pessimistic about their finances. Many people expect their financial situation to get worse, not better.

[ЭКРАН: сплит-скрин: «economy data» зелёные стрелки и «how people feel» красные.]

And here's what's strange: at various points in recent years, standard economic data like unemployment and wages looked decent. But people still felt bad. Some commentators even gave it a name: the "vibecession." A recession in vibes, not necessarily in the data.

[ЭКРАН: маскот задумчиво: «So who's right?»]

So who's right, the data or the feelings? The answer is: both. Let's look at why.

### 2:00 — Price memory

[ЭКРАН: ценник: «old price» перечёркнут, рядом «new price» больше.]

Reason one: price memory. Your brain remembers what things used to cost.

[ЭКРАН: яйца, аренда, кофе, страховка авто: старые и новые цены.]

After the big inflation spike a few years ago, prices for many everyday things jumped. Inflation has cooled since then, but here's the catch: lower inflation means prices are rising more slowly. It doesn't mean they go back down.

[ЭКРАН: лестница цен: ступенька вверх и дальше ровно, но уже выше.]

So even if your paycheck has caught up, every time you buy something, your brain compares it to the old price and says, "This is ridiculous." That small sting happens dozens of times a week.

[ЭКРАН: продукты и бензин крупно, телевизоры маленьким шрифтом.]

And we notice price increases most on things we buy often, like food, gas and coffee, while price drops on things like TVs or electronics barely register.

### 3:15 — Lifestyle creep

[ЭКРАН: зарплата растёт, рядом растут подписки, доставка, машина подороже.]

Reason two: lifestyle creep.

When we earn more, we usually spend more. A nicer apartment. A newer car. More delivery. A few more subscriptions. Each upgrade feels small and well-deserved.

[ЭКРАН: «new normal» табличка, маскот привыкает.]

But very quickly, the upgrades become your new normal. The raise is gone, absorbed into a slightly fancier life, and you feel just as tight as before. Sometimes tighter.

[ЭКРАН: фиксированные расходы растут как плющ.]

The dangerous part is when upgrades become fixed costs, like a bigger rent or car payment. Those are hard to undo when money gets tight.

### 4:15 — The comparison trap

[ЭКРАН: лента соцсетей: отпуска, новые машины, ремонты; под ними скрытые счета.]

Reason three: comparison.

Social media shows everyone's vacation, new car and kitchen remodel. It doesn't show their credit card balance, their buy now, pay later plans, or the money their parents gave them.

[ЭКРАН: маскот сравнивает свою «закулису» с чужой «витриной».]

So we compare our behind-the-scenes to everyone else's highlight reel. And of course we feel like we're falling behind.

[ЭКРАН: «reference group» расширяется от соседей до всего интернета.]

In the past, people compared themselves to neighbors and coworkers. Now we compare ourselves to millions of people online, including some of the richest people on Earth. That's a game nobody wins.

### 5:15 — The big-ticket squeeze

[ЭКРАН: три огромных ценника: дом, медицина, образование.]

Reason four is very real, and it's not just psychology: big-ticket costs.

[ЭКРАН: дом с ценником, ипотечная ставка, маскот смотрит снизу вверх.]

Housing is the biggest. Home prices rose dramatically, and mortgage rates went up from their old lows. For many younger people, buying a first home feels much harder than it did for their parents at the same age.

[ЭКРАН: страховка, медицина, детский сад, образование.]

Add childcare, health insurance and education, and the costs of the "normal" milestones of adult life have grown a lot.

[ЭКРАН: бюджет: повседневное «OK», но мечты недоступны.]

So someone might be doing fine on day-to-day expenses, and still feel broke, because the big goals they were raised to expect seem out of reach. That's not imaginary. That's a real squeeze.

### 6:15 — Feeling broke vs being broke

[ЭКРАН: две карточки разводятся в стороны: «feeling broke» и «being broke».]

So here's the key idea: feeling broke and being broke are two different problems, and they need different fixes.

[ЭКРАН: «being broke»: доходы меньше расходов, долг растёт.]

Being broke means your income doesn't cover your expenses, or your debt keeps growing. That needs action: cutting costs, increasing income or getting help with debt.

[ЭКРАН: «feeling broke»: всё вроде нормально, но тревожно.]

Feeling broke means your finances might be OK, or even improving, but stress, comparison and price shock make it feel bad. That needs clarity, not panic.

[ЭКРАН: туман, маскот с фонариком.]

And the problem is, most people don't actually know which one they are, because they never look at the full picture. So let's fix that.

[ЭКРАН: «Your money story», детские воспоминания: родители спорят о деньгах.]

There's one more layer: your money story. How your family talked about money growing up shapes how you feel about it today. If you grew up hearing "we can't afford that" all the time, you might feel broke even with a healthy savings account. If money was never discussed, you might feel lost and anxious about every decision.

[ЭКРАН: маскот разговаривает с другом за кофе.]

Talking about money with a trusted friend or partner can help a lot. You'll often find that others feel exactly the same way, and that alone makes the feeling lighter.

[ЭКРАН: «Quick recap», карточки складываются в стопку.]

Quick recap. Americans feel broke because of price memory, lifestyle creep, constant comparison and real pressure from big costs like housing. Some of that is feeling, and some of it is reality. The first step is figuring out which one is yours.

### 7:15 — Five steps to clarity

[ЭКРАН: пять карточек появляются по очереди.]

Here are five simple steps.

[ЭКРАН: 1: «Net worth» = assets − debts.]

One: calculate your net worth. Add up what you own, like savings and investments, and subtract what you owe. Do it once a month. The trend matters more than the number.

[ЭКРАН: 2: «Income − expenses», график за 3 месяца.]

Two: look at three months of income versus spending. Are you saving anything? Even a little?

[ЭКРАН: 3: «Fixed costs %», круговая диаграмма.]

Three: check your fixed costs. Rent, car, insurance, subscriptions. If they eat most of your income, that's where the real pressure is coming from.

[ЭКРАН: 4: телефон, отписка от аккаунтов «lifestyle».]

Four: curate your feed. Unfollow accounts that make you feel bad about your life.

[ЭКРАН: 5: копилка с надписью «1 month».]

And five: build one month of expenses as a cushion. Nothing reduces money anxiety faster than knowing you could handle a surprise bill.

[ЭКРАН: маскот выходит из тумана, график net worth плавно ползёт вверх.]

[ЭКРАН: маскот через три месяца: тот же график, но лицо спокойнее.]

Here's what usually happens when people do these five steps. Some discover they're actually doing better than they thought: their net worth is slowly growing, and the anxiety eases. Others discover a real problem, like fixed costs that are too high or debt that's growing. That's scary, but it's also the first step to fixing it. Either way, knowing beats guessing.

Track your net worth, not your mood. Feelings are real, but they're not a financial plan.
