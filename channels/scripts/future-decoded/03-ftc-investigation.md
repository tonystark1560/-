## Сценарий

### 0:00 — The letters

[ЭКРАН: два конверта с печатью падают на два стола в разных офисах.]

On October 14, 2026, according to reporting by Axios, two of the most important AI companies in the world received official letters from the US government.

The companies were OpenAI, the maker of ChatGPT, and Anthropic, the maker of Claude. The sender was the Federal Trade Commission.

[ЭКРАН: маскот открывает конверт, внутри длинный список вопросов.]

The letters reportedly ask about security incidents, the safeguards these companies use, and how they handle people's privacy.

[ЭКРАН: тёмная сцена, заголовки новостей.]

Now, this doesn't mean anyone did anything illegal. But it does mean that the AI industry just entered a new phase. Let's decode what's going on.

### 0:50 — Who is the FTC?

[ЭКРАН: здание агентства в Вашингтоне, щит с надписью «consumer protection».]

First, who is the FTC? The Federal Trade Commission is a US government agency with two big jobs: protecting consumers from unfair or deceptive business practices, and keeping competition fair.

[ЭКРАН: примеры: фальшивая реклама, утечка данных, скрытые платежи.]

If a company lies in its ads, hides fees, or doesn't protect your personal data the way it promised, the FTC can step in.

[ЭКРАН: стопка прошлых дел против крупных техкомпаний.]

Over the years, it has taken action against some of the biggest tech companies on things like privacy and data security. Some of those cases ended with major settlements and years of strict rules the companies had to follow.

[ЭКРАН: стрелка от старых дел к новому: «AI».]

So when the FTC starts asking AI companies hard questions, people pay attention.

### 2:00 — What's a civil investigative demand?

[ЭКРАН: документ «Civil Investigative Demand», ключевые слова подсвечиваются.]

The letters are reportedly what's called a "civil investigative demand," or CID. Sounds scary, but let's break it down.

"Civil" means it's not a criminal case. Nobody's being arrested.

"Investigative" means the FTC is gathering information.

"Demand" means the company has to answer. It's not a friendly request. The company must hand over documents and answer questions, usually under oath.

[ЭКРАН: аналогия: врач делает анализы, а не ставит диагноз.]

Think of it like a doctor ordering tests. Ordering a test doesn't mean you're sick. But the doctor wants to look more closely.

[ЭКРАН: развилка: «nothing», «settlement», «lawsuit».]

After a CID, things can go several ways. The FTC might find nothing and close the matter. It might negotiate a settlement where the company agrees to change certain practices. Or, in some cases, it might take the company to court. Often, investigations like this take months, or even years.

### 3:15 — What they're asking about

[ЭКРАН: три большие карточки: Security, Safeguards, Privacy.]

According to the reports, the letters focus on three areas.

[ЭКРАН: карточка Security: замок со взломанной дужкой.]

One: security incidents. AI companies hold huge amounts of data: user conversations, business documents, code. Regulators want to know if there have been breaches or leaks, and how they were handled.

[ЭКРАН: карточка Safeguards: щит, фильтр, кнопка «report».]

Two: safeguards. What do these companies do to stop their AI from being misused? From helping with scams, to harmful advice, to protecting kids. As AI gets more powerful, these questions get bigger.

[ЭКРАН: карточка Privacy: окно чата, текст превращается в нули и единицы и уходит в облако.]

And three: privacy. When you type something into a chatbot, what happens to it? Is it stored? For how long? Is it used to train future models? Who inside the company can see it?

[ЭКРАН: маскот задумчиво смотрит на свой чат.]

These are questions a lot of regular users have been asking too.

[ЭКРАН: «Why security is harder for AI», схема с новыми типами атак.]

And security for AI companies is especially tricky. They face all the normal threats any tech company faces, like hackers and leaked passwords. But they also face new ones. People try to trick AI models into revealing information they shouldn't. Attackers might try to steal the model itself, which can be worth billions. And as AI agents start taking actions on their own, a security mistake can mean an AI doing something real in the world.

[ЭКРАН: огромная стопка документов, которые нужно собрать в ответ.]

For the companies, answering a CID is a huge job. Lawyers and engineers spend weeks collecting documents, internal reports and records. It's expensive and distracting, even if nothing comes of it.

### 4:45 — It's not the first time

[ЭКРАН: таймлайн: 2023 → 2025 → 2026.]

This isn't the first time the FTC has looked at AI.

[ЭКРАН: 2023 год, отметка «OpenAI CID».]

Back in 2023, the FTC reportedly opened an investigation into OpenAI, asking about how ChatGPT handled personal data and whether it could make false statements about real people.

[ЭКРАН: 2025 год, «AI companions», иконки чат-ботов-компаньонов.]

In 2025, the FTC launched a broader inquiry into AI chatbots that act as companions, asking several big tech companies how they protect children and teens. That came after growing concern about young people forming emotional bonds with chatbots, and after lawsuits against companion apps.

[ЭКРАН: мир: Европа, Великобритания, Азия, у каждого свои правила ИИ.]

And it's not just the US. The European Union has passed its own big AI law, and governments from the UK to Asia are writing rules of their own. AI companies now have to deal with regulators all over the world.

[ЭКРАН: надпись «Wild West → rules».]

The bigger picture: AI is moving from a "wild west" phase into a phase with rules. That's normal for any powerful technology. Cars got seatbelt laws. Food got safety inspections. The internet got privacy rules. AI is next.

### 6:00 — What could happen next

[ЭКРАН: три двери с табличками.]

So what happens now? There are a few possibilities.

[ЭКРАН: дверь 1: «Nothing».]

Door one: the companies answer the questions, the FTC is satisfied, and the matter quietly closes.

[ЭКРАН: дверь 2: «New rules for one company».]

Door two: settlements. Companies agree to things like stronger security audits, clearer privacy settings or limits on how they use data. These agreements can last for many years.

[ЭКРАН: дверь 3: «Industry rules».]

Door three: the findings shape bigger rules for the whole AI industry, through new regulations or laws from Congress.

[ЭКРАН: маскот пожимает плечами, рядом «?».]

It's too early to say which door we'll get. But either way, every AI company is now watching closely, because what happens to the biggest players often sets the standard for everyone else.

### 7:15 — What it means for you

[ЭКРАН: окно чата, маскот печатает, потом стирает.]

So what does this mean for you, the person typing into a chatbot? Here's the practical takeaway.

[ЭКРАН: чек-лист появляется по пунктам.]

One: treat what you type into any AI like it could be stored. Don't paste in passwords, full credit card numbers, or other people's private information.

Two: check the settings. Most major AI apps let you control whether your chats are used for training and how long they're kept. Take two minutes and look.

Three: for work, use the business version your company approves, not your personal account. Those versions often come with stronger privacy promises.

[ЭКРАН: шерифская звезда появляется на маскоте.]

[ЭКРАН: «Quick recap», три карточки складываются в стопку.]

Quick recap. The FTC reportedly sent civil investigative demands to OpenAI and Anthropic. That's a formal request for information, not an accusation of wrongdoing. The questions focus on security incidents, safeguards and privacy. It's part of a bigger shift, with regulators in the US and around the world paying much closer attention to AI. And the outcome could range from nothing at all, to settlements, to new rules for the whole industry.

The fact that regulators are asking questions is actually good news for users. It means someone is checking. Big AI just got a little less wild west.
