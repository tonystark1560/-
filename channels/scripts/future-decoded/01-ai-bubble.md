## Сценарий

### 0:00 — The bad week

[ЭКРАН: маскот смотрит на биржевое табло, все тикеры краснеют.]

In mid-October 2026, AI stocks had a really bad week. The biggest AI and chip companies, the ones that had been going up and up for years, suddenly dropped hard, wiping out enormous amounts of value in just a few days.

[ЭКРАН: заголовки: «Is the AI bubble bursting?», «AI selloff».]

And immediately, everyone started asking the same question: is this it? Is the AI bubble finally popping?

[ЭКРАН: маскот надувает шар «AI», шар дрожит.]

Today, we're going to decode that question. What a bubble actually is. Why AI looks like one. Why it might not be. And what it means for you, even if you've never bought a single stock.

### 1:00 — What is a bubble?

[ЭКРАН: тюльпан на старинной гравюре, рядом ценник растёт.]

Let's start simple. A bubble is when the price of something rises far above what it's really worth, because people expect the price to keep rising.

The classic example is tulip mania in the Netherlands in the 1600s, when some rare tulip bulbs reportedly sold for the price of a house. People weren't buying tulips to plant them. They were buying them to sell to someone else for more.

[ЭКРАН: цикл: «hype → buying → higher prices → more hype».]

Bubbles feed themselves. Prices go up, so people get excited. People get excited, so they buy. They buy, so prices go up. Until one day, there are no new buyers left. And then the whole thing reverses.

[ЭКРАН: шар лопается, конфетти превращаются в падающие монеты.]

The tricky part is that, from inside a bubble, it almost never feels like one. It feels like the future.

### 2:15 — Why AI looks like a bubble

[ЭКРАН: ряды дата-центров до горизонта, счётчик расходов.]

So why are people worried about AI? Reason number one: spending.

The biggest tech companies are spending staggering amounts of money building data centers, buying chips and securing electricity to power AI. And they keep raising those budgets. Some analysts estimate that the AI build-out could need well over a trillion dollars in outside financing over the next few years.

[ЭКРАН: весы: на одной чаше огромная гора затрат, на другой маленькая стопка выручки.]

Reason number two: revenue hasn't caught up. AI products are growing fast, but the money coming in from AI is still small compared to the money going out. Investors are betting that the gap will close. If it doesn't close fast enough, they'll get nervous.

[ЭКРАН: круг стрелок: компания-чип → инвестирует в ИИ-стартап → стартап покупает чипы → снова.]

Reason number three: circular deals. Chip makers and cloud companies are investing billions into AI startups, which then spend that money buying chips and cloud services from… the same companies. It can make demand look stronger than it is.

[ЭКРАН: логотипы-заглушки стартапов с «AI» в названии сыплются как конфетти.]

And reason number four: hype. Startups add "AI" to their pitch and their valuations jump. Companies mention AI in earnings calls again and again. When a word becomes magic, that's usually a warning sign.

[ЭКРАН: весы: ценник «$100» против одной монетки «$1 profit», надпись «perfect future».]

Here's a simple way to think about it. When you buy a stock, you're buying a slice of a company's future profits. If investors pay a hundred dollars today for a company that earns one dollar a year, they're betting that profit will grow a lot, and fast. That's fine when the growth shows up. It's a problem when it doesn't. In a bubble, prices stop pricing in a great future, and start pricing in a perfect one.

### 4:00 — Why AI might not be

[ЭКРАН: маскот переворачивает карточку, на обороте «BUT…».]

But here's the other side. And it's a strong one.

First: AI is actually being used. Hundreds of millions of people use AI chatbots. Programmers use AI to write code. Companies use it for customer support, research, design and much more. This isn't a technology looking for a purpose.

[ЭКРАН: график прибыли крупных техкомпаний, он растёт.]

Second: the biggest spenders are some of the most profitable companies in history. During the dot-com bubble, many companies had no profits at all and were burning borrowed money. Today, a lot of the AI spending comes from giants that make huge profits from other businesses, like search, cloud and software.

[ЭКРАН: чип светится, рядом кривая улучшения моделей.]

Third: the technology keeps getting better and cheaper. Every year, AI models can do more for less money. That opens up new uses that weren't possible before.

[ЭКРАН: надпись «bubble ≠ fake».]

And here's the key idea: a bubble doesn't mean the technology is fake. It means the prices got ahead of reality. Both things can be true at once: AI can be world-changing, and AI stocks can still be overpriced.

[ЭКРАН: продавец лопат у золотой горы превращается в завод чипов.]

There's one more piece of the puzzle: the shovel sellers. In a gold rush, the people selling shovels often get rich no matter who finds gold. In the AI rush, the shovels are chips. The companies that design and make AI chips have seen their value explode, because every AI company needs huge numbers of them.

[ЭКРАН: стрелка: «AI spending ↓» → «chip sales ↓» → «stocks ↓».]

But that also makes them the most sensitive to any slowdown. If the big tech companies decide to spend even a little less on data centers next year, chip makers feel it immediately. That's a big reason why AI stocks can move so sharply on news that sounds small.

[ЭКРАН: электростанции, линии электропередач, счётчик мегаватт.]

And then there's power. AI data centers use enormous amounts of electricity. In some places, the limit on AI isn't money or chips anymore, it's whether the grid can deliver enough energy. Utilities and power companies have suddenly become part of the AI story too.

[ЭКРАН: паутина связей между компаниями.]

All of this means the AI economy is deeply connected. When one part sneezes, the rest can catch a cold.

### 5:30 — The dot-com lesson

[ЭКРАН: ретро-монитор, модем, браузер 1999 года.]

We've been here before. In the late 1990s, the internet was the future. And it was! Everyone who said the internet would change the world was right.

[ЭКРАН: график Nasdaq взлетает и падает со скалы.]

But in 2000, the bubble burst anyway. The Nasdaq fell nearly 80 percent from its peak. Hundreds of internet companies disappeared.

[ЭКРАН: карточка интернет-магазина товаров для животных: «IPO Feb 2000» → штамп «CLOSED Nov 2000».]

Pets.com, an online pet supply store with a famous sock puppet mascot, went public in February 2000. Nine months later, it shut down.

[ЭКРАН: корона «most valuable company, March 2000», затем график акции падает.]

And it wasn't just the weak companies. Cisco, which made the equipment that powered the internet, briefly became the most valuable company in the world in March 2000. Its products were real. Its customers were real. Its profits were real. But its stock had run so far ahead that over the next two years it lost most of its value anyway. Even a great company can be a terrible investment at the wrong price.

[ЭКРАН: маленькая коробка Amazon выбирается из обломков и растёт.]

And yet, Amazon survived. Google, founded right in the middle of the bubble, became a giant. The internet kept growing. Online shopping, search, social media, all of it happened, just a few years later than investors expected, and mostly by different companies than they expected.

[ЭКРАН: две двери: «the tech wins» и «most early companies lose».]

That's the pattern. The technology wins. Many of the early companies lose. And timing is everything.

### 6:45 — Warning signs

[ЭКРАН: приборная панель с тремя датчиками.]

So how will we know if AI is really in trouble? Here are three gauges to watch.

[ЭКРАН: датчик 1: «Spending cuts».]

Gauge one: spending cuts. If the big tech companies start cutting their AI budgets, that means they don't see the return. That would be a big deal, because a lot of other companies depend on that spending.

[ЭКРАН: датчик 2: «Customers».]

Gauge two: paying customers. Are businesses actually paying for AI and renewing their subscriptions, or just testing it and moving on? Real, repeat revenue is the best sign that something is not a bubble.

[ЭКРАН: датчик 3: «Debt».]

Gauge three: debt. Bubbles get really dangerous when they're built on borrowed money. If more and more of the AI build-out is paid for with loans instead of profits, the risk goes up. Because when debt goes bad, the damage spreads.

[ЭКРАН: график волнами: резкое падение, отскок, ещё одно падение.]

And if it does pop, it probably won't be one dramatic day. Bubbles usually deflate in waves: a sharp drop, a hopeful rebound, then another drop as the weakest companies run out of money. The strongest companies keep building. And a few years later, people look back and wonder how they ever doubted the technology, or how they ever paid those prices.

### 8:00 — What it means for you

[ЭКРАН: обычный человек смотрит на свой пенсионный счёт, внутри логотипы техкомпаний.]

Now, why should you care, even if you don't trade stocks? Because the biggest tech companies now make up a big chunk of the main US stock indexes. If you have a 401(k) or an index fund, a part of your retirement is probably riding on AI.

[ЭКРАН: маскот спокойно сидит, вокруг него бушует график.]

That's not a reason to panic or to sell everything. It's a reason to know what you own, keep your investments diversified, and not bet money you can't afford to lose on whatever's the hottest thing this month. This isn't financial advice, just the boring, proven stuff.

[ЭКРАН: корзина с яйцами; индексный фонд как сетка из сотни квадратиков, один краснеет, остальные держатся.]

Diversified just means not putting all your eggs in one basket. A broad index fund owns hundreds of companies across many industries, so if one part of the market crashes, it doesn't take everything down with it. And if you're investing for decades, the most powerful tool usually isn't timing the market perfectly. It's time in the market: investing regularly, through the ups and downs, instead of panic-selling at the bottom.

[ЭКРАН: шар лопается, внутри остаётся маленький светящийся чип.]

So, is the AI bubble about to pop? Maybe parts of it will. Some companies are almost certainly overvalued. But if history is any guide, the technology itself isn't going anywhere. The question isn't whether AI is real. It's who survives the shakeout.
