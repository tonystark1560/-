## Сценарий

### 0:00 — Lock and key

[ЭКРАН: сундук с чипами, нейросетями и кодом. Тяжёлый замок защёлкивается.]

China just took a step toward putting its AI under lock and key.

[ЭКРАН: список из 23 строк быстро прокручивается.]

Chinese government ministries have proposed adding 23 AI technologies to the country's technology export control list. That list includes large language models, the kind of AI behind chatbots, as well as AI agents and advanced chip design.

[ЭКРАН: маскот стоит между двумя растущими стенами.]

For years, it was mostly the United States restricting technology going to China. Now China is building walls of its own. So what does this actually mean, and why does it matter to you? Let's decode it.

### 0:50 — What is export control?

[ЭКРАН: граница, таможня, коробки с технологиями проходят через сканер.]

First, what are export controls? They're rules that limit what technology a country allows to leave its borders, or who it can be sold to.

[ЭКРАН: примеры: военная техника, ядерные материалы, шифрование.]

Countries have used them for a long time, especially for military equipment and "dual-use" technology, which means things that can be used for both civilian and military purposes.

[ЭКРАН: иконка «license required».]

Export control doesn't always mean a total ban. Often it means you need special government permission, a license, before you can sell or share that technology with foreign companies.

[ЭКРАН: ИИ-чип помещается в ту же категорию, что и ракета.]

And here's the big shift: AI is now being treated like a strategic technology, in the same category as advanced weapons and nuclear tech. That tells you how important governments think it is.

### 2:00 — What China wants to protect

[ЭКРАН: три карточки: Large Models, AI Agents, Chip Design.]

According to the proposal, the 23 technologies include three big areas.

[ЭКРАН: карточка LLM: окно чата с иероглифами и английским.]

Large models: the giant AI systems that power chatbots and many AI tools. Chinese companies have built some very capable models, and some of them have been released openly for anyone to download and use.

[ЭКРАН: карточка агентов: роботы, работающие сами.]

AI agents: systems that can take actions on their own, which we've covered on this channel before.

[ЭКРАН: карточка чипов: схема процессора под лупой.]

And advanced chip design: the know-how to design the processors that AI runs on.

[ЭКРАН: замок на сундуке, рядом надпись «proposed».]

Important detail: this is a proposal. It still has to go through the process before it becomes final, and the details could change. But the direction is clear.

### 3:15 — How we got here: the chip war

[ЭКРАН: таймлайн с 2018 года, по нему движется маленький чип.]

To understand this move, we need to go back a few years.

[ЭКРАН: 2022: США закрывают ворота для продвинутых чипов.]

Starting around 2022, the United States introduced strict rules to limit China's access to the most advanced AI chips, and to the machines needed to make them. The goal was to slow down China's progress in AI, especially for military uses.

[ЭКРАН: завод в Нидерландах, огромная литографическая машина.]

These rules also involved allies. Some of the most important chipmaking machines in the world are made in the Netherlands and Japan, and they were pressured to limit sales to China too.

[ЭКРАН: Китай строит свои фабрики, свои чипы, свои модели.]

China's response was to invest massively in its own chip industry and its own AI. And Chinese companies surprised many people by building strong AI models even with limited access to the best chips, by being very efficient.

[ЭКРАН: Китай ограничивает экспорт редких металлов, магнитов.]

China also has its own powerful cards to play. It dominates the production and processing of many rare earth minerals and other materials that are critical for electronics, and it has restricted exports of some of them in the past.

[ЭКРАН: шахматная доска, ход за ходом.]

So this new AI export proposal is another move in a long chess game.

### 5:00 — Why now?

[ЭКРАН: маскот держит лупу над картой.]

So why would China want to restrict exports of its own AI? There are a few likely reasons.

[ЭКРАН: причина 1: сейф с надписью «know-how».]

One: protecting its lead. China now has world-class AI talent and models. It doesn't want that know-how flowing freely to competitors.

[ЭКРАН: причина 2: весы переговоров между двумя странами.]

Two: bargaining power. Having its own controls gives China something to negotiate with in trade talks with the US and others.

[ЭКРАН: причина 3: щит национальной безопасности.]

Three: national security. Just like the US, China sees advanced AI as a strategic and military technology.

[ЭКРАН: причина 4: стрелка внутрь страны.]

And four: keeping control at home. Export rules also give the government more oversight of what its own tech companies do with AI abroad.

[ЭКРАН: «Can you even stop software?» — код летит по интернету через границы.]

But here's a tricky question: can you actually stop AI from crossing borders? Chips are physical. You can stop a shipment at customs. But an AI model is just a file. It can be copied, uploaded and downloaded in minutes.

[ЭКРАН: открытая модель, которую уже скачали миллионы.]

Once an open model has been released and downloaded around the world, there's no way to pull it back. That's why export controls on AI usually focus on future releases, on the know-how of how to build models, and on cooperation between companies, not on files that are already out there.

[ЭКРАН: исследователи на конференции, вокруг них появляются невидимые стены.]

Controls can also affect people. Researchers may need permission to share certain work with foreign partners. International collaborations can get more complicated. Science has always moved fastest when ideas flow freely, so these rules come with a real cost.

### 6:15 — Two AI worlds

[ЭКРАН: экран делится пополам: два разных набора приложений, чипов и правил.]

Here's the big-picture worry: the world could split into two separate AI ecosystems.

[ЭКРАН: левая половина: американские модели и чипы; правая: китайские.]

On one side, AI models, chips and cloud services from the US and its allies. On the other side, a Chinese AI stack: Chinese chips, Chinese models, Chinese apps.

[ЭКРАН: страны мира выбирают, к какой стороне подключиться.]

Other countries might end up having to choose which side to build on. Some would use American AI, some Chinese, and some would try to use both.

[ЭКРАН: две версии одного ответа ИИ, разные по содержанию.]

And because AI systems are trained on different data and follow different rules, two AI worlds could also mean two different versions of "the truth," with AI answering sensitive questions differently depending on where it was made.

[ЭКРАН: плюсы и минусы на весах.]

Some experts say competition will push both sides to innovate faster. Others worry it makes cooperation on AI safety much harder, at the exact moment the technology is getting more powerful.

### 7:30 — What it means for you

[ЭКРАН: обычный человек со смартфоном, на экране ИИ-приложение.]

So what does this mean for you? Probably not much tomorrow. But over the next few years, it could affect a lot.

[ЭКРАН: ценник на гаджеты растёт.]

Prices: trade fights over chips and materials can make electronics more expensive.

[ЭКРАН: приложение недоступно в одной стране, доступно в другой.]

Access: some AI apps and models may be available in one country but not another.

[ЭКРАН: открытая модель скачивается, затем на ней появляется замок.]

Open models: some of the most popular freely available AI models have come from Chinese labs. If those become harder to share, that changes what developers around the world can build.

[ЭКРАН: шахматная доска с двумя фигурами-флагами, маскот наблюдает.]

[ЭКРАН: «Quick recap», карточки складываются в стопку.]

Quick recap. China has proposed putting 23 AI technologies, including large models, agents and chip design, under export control. It's the latest move in a long tech rivalry that started with US limits on advanced chips. Software is much harder to control than hardware. And the biggest risk is a world split into two separate AI systems, with different chips, different models and different rules.

The AI race isn't just between companies anymore. It's between countries. And the rules being written right now will shape what AI you use for years to come.
